LucidLyra Feed-Forward Generative 3D Scene Reconstruction and Understanding via Multi-Teacher Distillation

Anonymous authors

TL;DR: LucidLyra turns a single image into a 3D Gaussian scene you can explore from new viewpoints, query with language, and edit object by object, with a time-conditioned extension for dynamic scenes from monocular video.

Abstract

We present LucidLyra, a framework that makes generatively reconstructed 3D scenes directly queryable in a feed-forward manner from video latents. While generative 3D reconstruction can synthesize virtual environments from a single image beyond the observed view, downstream applications in robotics and physical AI require more than geometry and appearance: scenes must also represent semantics and object identity. Existing 3D understanding approaches, however, require captured multi-view scenes, have limited generalization capabilities, or rely on expensive per-scene optimization. To bridge this gap, our method distills complementary knowledge from a camera-controlled video diffusion model and frozen 2D vision foundation models into 3D Gaussians that jointly encode geometry, appearance, open-vocabulary semantics, and class-agnostic instance cues. The resulting scenes support novel-view rendering, language queries, and object grouping without per-scene optimization at test time, while training requires neither manual 3D annotations nor captured multi-view datasets. Furthermore, our latent formulation naturally extends to monocular video, reconstructing structured dynamic 3D scenes across time. Our method outperforms baselines combining state-of-the-art 3DGS segmentation approaches with generative 3D scene reconstruction across diverse evaluation domains, without compromising reconstruction quality. By turning renderable but unstructured outputs into queryable 3D worlds, LucidLyra enables downstream applications including 3D scene editing, asset extraction, and robotic simulation.

Method

A camera-controlled video model generates multiple views from a single image, while frozen 2D semantic and tracking models provide aligned training targets without manual 3D annotations. We distill these targets into a Gaussian decoder that combines generated video latents, camera rays, and features from the input image to predict geometry, appearance, semantic features, and instance embeddings in one forward pass without per-scene optimization. For monocular video, time conditioning reconstructs a separate 3D scene at each queried time.
